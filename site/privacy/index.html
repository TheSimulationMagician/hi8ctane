PRIVACY

Draft. Francisco checks it, and a lawyer reviews it before the app launches. Last updated 8 November 2026.

Who we are

Hi8ctane is run by Francisco Arredondo, Amsterdam, the Netherlands. Questions and requests: hello@hi8ctane.com.

What we keep and why

  • Getting in: your email, your city and your founding plate number. To keep your place and your plate, and to tell you when crews start near you.
  • Your rider profile: your rider name, sports and the level you picked in each, your start point, how far you'll go and how you get there. To match you with crews at your level that you can reach.
  • New cities: the cities you said you're in, and if you'll captain, your sport and days. To count riders and captains per city and open it when there are enough.
  • Founding Night: your session, pickup hub, when you ticked the waiver, and whether photos of you are fine. To run the day and the night.
  • Schools and rental shops: the name, spot, what you offer and how to reach you. To get back to you.
  • Where you came from: if you arrived through an ad or someone's link (for example ?ref=), we note that with your sign-up, to know which links work.
  • Crews: the crews you start or join, your level and how you get there (driving, a seat, your own way), and the crew chat. Only people in that crew see the names, the cars, the meeting note and the chat.
  • At the spot: when you check in and the reports you post (on, meh or off, your kite size or the ground). Your crew sees your name and note; everyone else sees the report without your name.
  • Settling up: what your car cost and, if you add them, a payment link or your Bizum number. Only the riders in your car see them; your Bizum number is never sent by email.
  • Alerts: the sports and cities you want an email about when a crew is posted. Every email has a stop link.
  • Plans: the plans you say you're in for (an MX day, a cable park), the day, how many of you and your note. We share the day, the group size and the note with the place and the others going, to set it up; never your email.

Your email is never shown to other riders. Later, other riders will see your rider name, level and plate.

In your browser

We set no advertising or tracking cookies. To keep you signed up from page to page, the site keeps a random key and your choices in your browser's local storage, and the weight you give for the kite size stays there too: it never leaves your phone. Clearing this site's data removes them; it doesn't delete your sign-up.

Alerts and push

An alert keeps the spots, wind, days and hours you picked, so we can tell you when the forecast matches. If you turn on push, your browser gives us an address for this site only (from Apple, Google or Mozilla's push service) and two keys to encrypt the message; we keep those with your email and nothing else about your phone. Messages are encrypted before they leave us. A push address the service says is gone is deleted; switching push off on this page or in your phone's settings stops it. Every alert email has a stop link. We keep which alerts went out for 30 days so you never get the same one twice.

Your cartoon

Only if you ask for one. When you tick the box and tap DRAW ME, the photo you picked is shrunk on your phone and sent to OpenAI, who draw it as a cartoon. We keep the cartoon only, never the photo. The cartoon is stored as a file with a random name and shown on your rider card (see below). Remove it in Me at any time; erasing your account removes it too.

Your rider profile and your phone number

"I'm looking": the days you say you're free for a sport, whether you need a seat and from which pick-up point, and your level. We use it to tell you, once per day, when a crew that fits goes up. Other riders and captains only see how many people are looking that day, never who. It's deleted the day after, or when you stop looking.

The session plan: if you're a crew's captain and tap "drop the pin where I am", your phone's location is read once and saved as the crew's meeting pin (only your crew sees it, and it has to be near the spot). We don't track where you are. When a crew moves, we keep whether you've said you're still in. When you hand a crew over, the next rider in line is asked to captain it. Captains can mark a no-show after a session; your card then shows how often you showed up (only once you've been in 3 sessions or more).

Counting what works: when you start, share, open or join a crew, tap directions, add it to your calendar or say you're looking, the site counts it with a random number made in your browser. Not your email, not your sign-in, not your IP address, and no cookies or outside trackers. We use the counts to see whether crews actually happen (for example, how long starting a crew takes). Clearing your browser's data gives you a new random number.

Signing in: if you've joined before, typing your email isn't enough to get into your crews on a new phone. We email you a sign-in link instead, so nobody else can open your crews by typing your email.

Your rider card: years riding, home spot, a line about yourself and Instagram are optional. You choose who sees it. If you pick "riders deciding to join", riders looking at a crew you're in see your card with your cartoon (if you made one), but without your name or your Instagram: your plate, level, years riding, home spot, your line and how much you've ridden (and, if they're signed in, how often you've ridden together). Names and Instagram are only for the riders in a crew you're in. Until you choose, or if you pick "only my crews" (you can change it any time in Me), outside your crews only the first letter of your name and your level show: no cartoon, no numbers. A phone number you share on a crew page is shown only to that crew. Captains can mark someone who didn't show up; only captains see that count. Erasing your account removes all of it.

Who's looking

While the page is open, it tells us once a minute that someone is looking, with a random number made in your browser. That number isn't your rider key and carries no email, name or IP address. We use it to show how many people are looking now and today, and to count visitors per day and region. The per-visitor records are deleted after 90 days; only the daily totals are kept.

Stopping spam

To stop bots filling the lists, we count sign-ups per visitor using a scrambled (salted hash) version of your IP address, never the address itself, and delete it after a day.

Who else is involved

  • Supabase stores the data, in the EU.
  • GitHub Pages serves the website; like any web host, it sees your IP address.
  • Open-Meteo provides the forecast: your browser asks it directly, so it sees your IP address, not who you are.
  • Google Fonts serves the fonts on a few older pages (Founding Night, cities, schools), so Google sees your IP address there. The app itself uses fonts we host.
  • Email: when we send you a sign-in link or news about your city, it goes out through our email provider.
  • Stripe handles top-ups, the Hi8 Pass and drivers' withdrawals: it gets your email and your payment or bank details, which we never see or keep. We keep what moved in and out of your wallet, for 7 years as bookkeeping rules require; if you ask us to delete your data, those records stay but no longer carry your name.
  • Places in a plan (a track, a cable park, a rental shop) get the day, group size and note of the riders in it, so they can have the bikes, gear or passes ready.

We never sell your data.

Legal basis

Doing what you asked (keeping your place, your plate, your RSVP), your consent (photos), and our legitimate interest in keeping the lists free of spam.

How long

Until you ask us to delete it, or 24 months after we last heard from you. The spam counters after a day.

Your rights

Ask for a copy, a correction or deletion at any time: email hello@hi8ctane.com from the address you signed up with, and we do it within 30 days. You can also complain to the Dutch data protection authority (Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens).

Age

Hi8ctane is for people aged 18 and over.
